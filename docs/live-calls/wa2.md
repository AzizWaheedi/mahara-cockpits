# Lead groups on the setter's personal WhatsApp: risks, mitigations and house rules

Written 2026-10-03 for the CEO's decision that "personal phone should be fine of the setter". Everything here was read-only research. VERIFIED means I opened the cited page and read it. UNVERIFIED means a search snippet, a secondary source or my own inference. The facts F1 to F19, the laws L1 to L7 and the prices C1 to C3 are listed with their links at the end.

## Summary

- **What the CEO chose.** Groups run from the setter's personal WhatsApp. That works today and costs nothing. It does move four things outside the company's control: the leads' numbers, the chat record, the setter's account standing and the setter's evenings.
- **Risk 1: a ban.** WhatsApp's consumer terms forbid "any non-personal use of our Services unless otherwise authorized by us" (F1). A ban locks the setter out of their whole personal WhatsApp, chats and backups included, and some bans are final (F3).
- **Risk 2: the setter leaves.** Chats and numbers stay on the setter's phone. Leads keep writing to a number the company cannot see.
- **Risk 3: nobody else can see.** HighLevel and the cockpit see nothing. Only the people in the group do.
- **Risk 4: the law.** About 65% of leads are Saudi. Under the Saudi data law (PDPL), marketing needs consent and an easy way out, and a breach that could cause harm must be reported within 72 hours (L1, L2). A lost phone may count as a breach.
- **One cheap fix covers most of this.** Buy a company phone with a KD 5 a month line and the free WhatsApp Business app, named "Mahara Media". Make it co-admin of every group. The CEO and the manager link it on WhatsApp Web. Year one costs about KD 109 (C1, C2, F14, F15).
- **Add house rules:**
  - Ask the lead first.
  - Never save a lead's number to personal contacts.
  - Make two co-admins at creation.
  - One group per demo, closed 2 days after the demo.
  - Quiet hours.
  - Report a lost phone within 1 hour.
- **Review at 30 days.** Decide then whether groups should be made from the company number, so the setter's personal number drops out.

## What changes compared with the earlier plan (r5, route C)

In route C no staff number reached the lead, and every message stayed in HighLevel. The personal-phone route reverses both:
- The setter's and the closer's numbers are visible to the lead and cannot be hidden (F4).
- No message lands in HighLevel, so the follow-up agent cannot see what was agreed.
- The official-line demo chat and the group could also message the same lead twice. Rule: while a group is open, the official-line demo chat for that appointment holds its steps. Email stays as the only fallback.

## 1. Who owns the leads' numbers and the chat history when the setter leaves

**What is true today**
- The leads' numbers already sit in HighLevel, which is the company's record.
- Personal data is not property. Under the Saudi law, Mahara is responsible for leads' data processed for its sales work (my reading of L1; UNVERIFIED as a formal legal opinion). The setter has no right to use these numbers for anything else, but the company has no technical control over a personal phone.
- Every group shows every member's number (F4). If the setter saves a lead as a contact, the number also goes into the setter's address book and personal cloud backup.
- Chat history lives on each member's phone. Deleting a group only erases it on the phone that deletes it. The lead keeps the history (F6).
- A removed member's name and number stay in the group's "Past members" list for up to 60 days (F6).
- When the setter leaves, they still hold:
  - every open group;
  - any one-to-one chats with leads;
  - the past-member lists.
- Leads who reply to the old number reach someone who no longer works for the company. The company never sees those replies.

**Mitigations**
1. **A company number in every group as admin** (section 5). The record and the admin rights stay with the company, whoever leaves.
2. **Never save a lead to personal contacts.**
   - Add the lead by typing the number. WhatsApp allows adding numbers that are not saved (F7).
   - Or send the invite through a click-to-chat link `wa.me/<number>`, which opens a chat without saving the number (F13).
3. **No one-to-one chats with leads from the personal number**, apart from the single message that carries the invite. Everything else goes in the group or on the official line.
4. **A written agreement before the first group:**
   - leads' numbers and chats are company confidential;
   - groups close on time;
   - at exit, the setter posts a handover line, exits and deletes every group;
   - the setter does not contact leads afterwards.

   Whether this clause is enforceable under Kuwaiti labour law is UNVERIFIED. A lawyer should check it.
5. **Exit checklist (the manager):**
   - Pull the open-group list from the cockpit.
   - Confirm the company number is admin in each group.
   - Add the new setter. When adding, WhatsApp can send up to 100 messages from the last 14 days as history (F7).
   - The leaving setter posts one line, then exits and deletes each group.
   - The manager marks each group as done in the cockpit and removes the cockpit seat.

## 2. If the setter's phone is lost, changed or banned

**Lost or stolen**
- WhatsApp cannot deactivate the account remotely. The only way to log the lost phone out is to block the SIM, get a new SIM with the same number and register again (F11).
- Until then, anyone holding the unlocked phone can read every group.
- For Saudi leads this may be a personal data breach. A breach that could cause harm goes to the Saudi data authority (SDAIA) within 72 hours (L1). Whether a locked, lost phone triggers this is UNVERIFIED, so the rules treat it as a possible breach.

Mitigations:
- A screen lock on the phone.
- WhatsApp's two-step verification PIN (I did not open that help page: UNVERIFIED).
- The setter tells the manager within 1 hour.
- The manager, as co-admin, removes the setter's number from open groups and posts one line in each.
- The setter blocks the SIM.
- The CEO decides on any notice.
- Closing groups on time keeps the number of exposed groups small.

**Changed number**
- WhatsApp tells every group when a number changes, whatever the notify setting (F10). Leads will see the new personal number.
- Backups made under the old number will not restore (F10).
- Rule: tell the manager first, and close or hand over open groups before changing.

**Banned**
- A ban blocks the whole account, and there is no access to chats or backups while it lasts. A review can be requested, but some bans are final. Only WhatsApp can lift one (F3).
- This work raises the ban risk in several ways:
  - business use on a consumer account (F1);
  - adding people who did not ask (F2);
  - the "Exit" and "Report" prompt that WhatsApp shows when a stranger adds someone to a group (F5);
  - a burst of adds, which can force a 24-hour wait (F7).
- If the setter is the only admin and drops out, WhatsApp makes a random member admin. That could be the lead (F6).

Mitigations:
- Ask on the call first. Prefer the invite link with "Approve new members" on (F8).
- Make the closer and the company number admins at creation.
- No broadcast lists, no copy-paste blasts and no modified WhatsApp apps (F2).
- At most 5 new groups a day from one phone. This is a house cap, not a WhatsApp number.
- After any warning or temporary ban, the setter moves to the company phone the same day. The company states in writing that it will never ask the setter to carry on with a personal account that is at risk.

## 3. Mixing personal and work life

**What a lead sees by default (F4)**
- The setter's phone number. It is always visible and cannot be hidden in a group.
- The profile photo, which anyone can see by default.
- "About", status and last seen, which contacts can see by default. Last seen also reaches people the setter has messaged.
- Read receipts, which are always on in groups.

**Risks**
- Messages at 23:00 and on Fridays.
- Leads making WhatsApp calls to the personal number.
- Leads seeing family photos.
- A lead writing privately weeks later.
- Pressure to answer at all hours.
- Personal safety and comfort.

**Mitigations**
- **Privacy settings for the setter and the closer** (F4):
  - profile photo: My contacts;
  - About: My contacts;
  - last seen and online: Nobody;
  - status: My contacts.

  Because leads are never saved as contacts, they see none of this. A neutral professional photo is the alternative.
- **The company identity goes on the group, not the person:** the group name, the Mahara logo as group photo, and a description saying who is who.
- **Hours:**
  - Staff start messages only 09:00 to 18:00 in the lead's local time, Sunday to Thursday.
  - Messages about the booked call can go up to 15 minutes before it, whatever the hour.
  - A lead who just wrote can get a reply until 21:00.
- **Mute work groups outside hours.** The closer owns replies. The manager sees unanswered messages the next morning on the company number.
- **WhatsApp calls from leads:** decline, write "I'll call you from our line" and call through Maqsam, so the call is recorded in HighLevel.
- **Harassment or inappropriate messages:** the setter may exit, block and report (F5), then tell the manager. The company backs the setter.
- **Fair pay for the device:** a phone allowance equal to the work line (KD 5 a month) while the personal phone is used. Get the setter's written yes, and offer the company phone at any time.
- **Cheaper separation if wanted:** a work eSIM on the same phone. WhatsApp allows two accounts on one phone, each with its own privacy settings (F19). The WhatsApp Business app can also run beside WhatsApp on a second number (F14).

## 4. Privacy and consent duties (practical level)

**One rule that meets the strictest country:**
- Get a clear yes from the lead, and record it.
- Use it for one purpose: this demo.
- Give an easy way out.
- Delete on time.
- Keep promotion out of the group.

WhatsApp asks for the same: get permission before adding someone to a group, and respect anyone who leaves (F2). The Business app rules require opt-in and respect for any opt-out request (F17, F18).

**Saudi Arabia (896 leads, about 65%)**
- The PDPL applies to organisations outside the Kingdom that process data of people in the Kingdom. Enforcement was expected from mid-September 2024. Fines go up to SAR 5 million (L1).
- Consent appears to be the only basis for marketing. Each purpose needs its own consent, and opting out must be easy (L1, L2).
- A 2025 draft amendment asks for consent to be documented. It was not final in May 2025, and its status now is UNVERIFIED (L3).
- Records of processing are kept for 5 years. Security must follow the national cybersecurity controls or recognised good practice (L1).
- Whether a group about a demo the lead booked counts as "marketing" or as service is UNVERIFIED. Treat it as needing consent.
- I could not open SDAIA's official text (L4).

**UAE (252 leads)**
- The federal data law (Decree-Law 45/2021) gives people the right to object to direct marketing. Its executive regulations were still unpublished in January 2025 (L5).
- Cabinet Resolution 56/2024 (L6):
  - applies to companies licensed in the UAE, free zones included;
  - bans individuals from telemarketing on personal numbers;
  - limits calls to 9:00 to 18:00 and requires consent;
  - per a Rouse note and DLA Piper, covers marketing messages sent through social apps.
- Whether Mahara holds a UAE licence is UNVERIFIED. If it does, promotional messages to UAE leads from a personal number would breach this rule.
- Either way, for UAE leads: only the booked call is discussed, and staff message only 9:00 to 18:00 UAE time.

**Kuwait (122 leads)**
- Kuwait has no general data protection law (L7).
- CITRA's 2024 privacy regulation covers CITRA-licensed telecom and IT providers, with a 24-hour breach notice. That Mahara is outside it is UNVERIFIED but likely.
- In practice the limits are WhatsApp's rules and the staff agreement.

**Qatar (71) and Bahrain (48)** have their own laws. I did not check them (UNVERIFIED).

**What to record in the cockpit for each group:**
- who asked;
- when;
- the words used;
- the lead's country;
- the closing date;
- the close reason.

This is the documented consent and the processing record.

## 7. Leaving and closing groups on time

**The close rule** (from r5). The group closes at whichever comes first:
- 2 days after the demo;
- the deal is won or lost;
- the lead is tagged `client`;
- the demo is cancelled with no new time within 2 days;
- the lead asks to stop, or leaves.

**Why on time protects everyone:**
- **The lead:** no stale group and no surprise messages later.
- **The setter:** fewer reports, so a lower ban risk (F5). Fewer chats sit on the personal phone if it is lost. Evenings come back.
- **The company:**
  - the talk returns to the official line, where HighLevel records it and the follow-up agent can work;
  - the show-rate test stays clean;
  - data is not kept longer than needed.

**How to close** (F6):
1. Post the closing line (section 8).
2. If the company number is not in the group, export the chat (F12) and attach it to the HighLevel contact.
3. Remove the lead, then the other members. Exit and choose "Exit and delete for me".
   - The lead keeps the history but nobody can send (F6).
   - Never just walk out: if the last admin leaves, WhatsApp makes a random member admin, and that could be the lead (F6).
4. Mark "Closed" in the cockpit with the reason.

A lead who leaves the group is never added back (F2).

## 8. What to tell a lead so it feels personal and useful, not spammy

All copy is English. The Arabic must be written under the aziz-kuwaiti-voice skill and reviewed by the CEO before use. Placeholders stand in for real first names.

**On the call (asking permission):**
"Would it help if I made a small WhatsApp group with you, me and {closer}, who will run your demo on {day}? We'll use it only for this call: the link, any questions and quick changes. We close it two days after the demo. Is that OK?"
- Record the yes in the cockpit.
- If the answer is no, use the official line only.

**Group setup:**
- **Name:** "{Company} + Mahara Media · demo {Sun 5 Oct}". The name limit is 100 characters (F9).
- **Photo:** the Mahara logo.
- **Permissions:** only admins edit group info (F9), and "Approve new members" is on (F8).
- **Description:** "For your Mahara demo on {day} at {time} ({country} time). {Setter} (booking) and {closer} (your host). We reply Sunday to Thursday, 9:00 to 18:00. This group closes on {date}. You can leave at any time."

**Messages:** at most 4 started by staff before the demo, plus replies.
1. **Welcome (setter):** "Hi {first name}, as promised, here's our group for {day}. {Closer} will host. Anything you want covered, drop it here."
2. **Prep (closer):** "Hi {first name}, {closer} here. To make the 45 minutes worth it, it helps to know roughly how many enquiries you get a month and where they come from. No need to prepare anything else."
3. **Day-before check:** "Quick check for tomorrow at {time} ({country} time). Still good?"
4. **Link, 15 minutes before:** "Here's the link: {short link}. See you in 15 minutes."

**Closing line:** "Thanks {first name}. We're closing this group now as planned. For anything else, message Mahara Media here: {official line}."

**Never in the group:**
- offers, discounts or case-study blasts;
- forwarded adverts;
- voice notes outside hours;
- asking for referrals;
- adding the lead's colleagues without that person's own yes.

## House rules for lead groups run from a rep's phone (one page)

**Before a group**
1. A group is only for a booked intro or demo. One group per call, never reused.
2. Ask on the call and get a clear yes. Mark it in the cockpit with the time and words. No yes, no group.
3. Saudi, UAE, Kuwaiti, Qatari or Bahraini leads all get the same rule. Nothing promotional goes in a group.

**Making it**
4. Do not save the lead to your contacts. Add by typing the number, or send the invite with `wa.me/<number>`.
5. At creation, make the closer and the Mahara Media number admins. Turn on "Approve new members". Only admins edit group info.
6. Use the standard name, logo and description. At most 5 new groups a day per phone.

**While it is open**
7. Start messages 09:00 to 18:00 in the lead's local time, Sunday to Thursday. The 15-minute link may go at any hour. Answer a lead who just wrote until 21:00.
8. At most 4 messages started by staff before the call. The closer owns replies. Answer within working hours.
9. No one-to-one chats with leads on your personal number. Decline WhatsApp calls and call back on Maqsam.
10. If a lead says stop or leaves: stop, mark it in the cockpit and never add them back.
11. No broadcast lists, no copy-paste blasts, no modified WhatsApp apps, and no screenshots of lead chats outside the company.

**Closing**
12. Close 2 days after the demo, or when the deal is won, lost or cancelled, whichever comes first.
13. Post the closing line. Remove the lead, then the others. Exit and delete. Mark it closed. Never walk out and leave the group running.

**Your phone and your privacy**
14. Set your profile photo, About and status to "My contacts", and last seen to "Nobody". Use a screen lock and the WhatsApp PIN.
15. Mute work groups outside hours. You are never expected to answer at night.
16. If a lead is rude or inappropriate: exit, block, report and tell the manager. The company backs you.

**Incidents**
17. Phone lost or stolen: tell the manager within 1 hour, then block the SIM. The manager removes your number from open groups.
18. Any WhatsApp warning, restriction or ban: tell the manager the same day. You move to the company phone and are never asked to carry on with a personal account that is at risk.
19. Changing your number: tell the manager first, and close or hand over open groups before you change.

**Leaving the company**
20. Before your last day: the manager adds your replacement to each open group, you post a handover line, then you exit and delete every group. You do not contact leads afterwards.

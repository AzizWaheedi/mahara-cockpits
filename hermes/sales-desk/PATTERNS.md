# What every closing call has in common

Read from four recorded calls: an interior design and fit-out firm (Saudi Arabia), a fit-out contractor (Saudi Arabia), a civil and fit-out firm (Qatar), and a supply and fit-out venture (Saudi Arabia). This is the evidence behind the template. If a future call contradicts something here, change the template rather than the individual proposal.

## The offer, as it was quoted

The figures a proposal prints come from `offer.json` and the closer's choice, and they are in the offer block of the skill above. What the four calls had in common:

- **The program, its term and its price were quoted identically in all four calls.**
- **The payment structure moves, the price does not.** In those calls the fee was split, half to begin and half after the client's first contract signed through the system. The fit-out contractor's reaction to that structure was the strongest moment in his call: it is proof we carry risk, and it beats a discount. Never discount. Whether a proposal carries a split is the closer's choice, made per proposal.
- **The deposit** reserves the start date and comes off the first payment.
- **Advertising** is paid by the client directly to the platforms, always its own line.
- **Timeline:** onboarding call, 7 to 10 days of build, meetings landing within 10 to 15 days.

## The funnel arithmetic never changes

At the agreed advertising budget: {{pitch.enquiries_month}} qualified enquiries a month, the best {{pitch.best_share_pct}} percent become {{pitch.meetings_month}} meetings, those become {{pitch.quotations_month}} quotations, and at a {{pitch.close_rate_pct}} percent close rate that is {{pitch.signed_month}} signed projects a month. Stated in the same words in every call.

## The five steps never change

Paid advertising, funnel filtration, the call centre, sales training and consultations, then reporting and CRM. Two marketing steps, two sales steps, one scalability step.

## The positioning never changes

Not a marketing agency, a growth partner. Engineering, construction and design companies only. GCC only. Measured in signed projects, never in followers, likes or reach. Every client is filtered on three things: they are the decision maker, they can fund the work, and they buy on value rather than on lowest price.

## The problem is the same problem

Four different companies, one shape:

- **Everything comes from referrals and repeat clients.** The fit-out contractor: 8 to 10 million riyals of contracts this year, not one client from marketing. The supply venture: relationships and nothing else. Another owner: back from two years dormant with no channel at all. Referrals are the best work and the one channel that cannot be turned up when a quarter goes quiet.
- **Nobody owns sales and marketing.** In every case it is the owner, between quotations, site visits and finance. The fit-out contractor named it precisely: self-employed rather than a business owner.
- **The enquiries that do arrive are unqualified.** Messages with no project, no budget, no decision maker. The fit-out contractor prices ten and half are not serious. The design firm met 18 people a month to sign four.
- **There are no numbers.** No record of enquiries, calls, meetings or quotations, so a good month cannot be told from a lucky one.
- **Capacity is idle.** The fit-out contractor was running at 60 to 70 percent and would take more tomorrow. Idle capacity is the cheapest thing a proposal can sell against.

## The objections are the same objections

- *We already get clients from referrals.* Answer with consistency, not volume: referrals cannot be forecast or scaled.
- *An agency wasted my time and money before.* Answer with the fixed term and the payment tied to a signed contract.
- *You have no office in my country.* One owner pressed hardest on this. Answer with market research per country, existing clients in that market, and results that came from a fully remote team.
- *How do you find people who actually have projects?* Answer with targeting plus the qualification funnel, and the worked example: our own ad is how they found us.
- *What if the volume is more than I can handle?* Answer with pace control, and with the filter that means meetings only happen with qualified buyers.
- *Can you lower the price?* No. Offer a payment plan instead, where the closer has agreed one.

## What actually changes per client

Six things, and only these:

1. Who they are: company, contact, market, and the kind of project they want more of.
2. Where their work comes from today, and what that costs them in consistency.
3. Their funnel numbers: enquiries, meetings, quotations, signed.
4. Their average project value.
5. Their net margin today, at the bottom of whatever range they quote.
6. Their goal, in their own words and their own currency.

Numbers 4 and 5 drive the return page by themselves. Enter them in the `roi` block of the deal's data file and the break-even maths writes itself.

## The two pages that have to be built from the call

Everything else in the template is the same pitch every time. These two are not, and they are where the proposal is won.

**The driver tree.** Their goal at the top, two branches beneath it, two sub-drivers under each. The branches must be genuinely separate and must together cover the whole goal. For a company with enquiries already arriving, the split is almost always *get more of them into a meeting* and *sign more of the meetings you already hold*, because the funnel multiplies those two rates and nothing else. For a company with no channel at all, it is *create enquiries where there are none* and *turn them into contracts*. If a third branch exists for a particular client, it belongs on the page.

**The cost of the gap.** Three layers, in their currency, per month:

1. **Cash already going out for nothing.** Advertising spent on the share of enquiries that never convert. Ad spend times (enquiries minus signed) divided by enquiries.
2. **The contract the same funnel could have signed.** One more project a month, at their average project value and the bottom of their own margin range. One, not three: the figure has to survive an argument.
3. **The load on the people.** Meetings and quotations a month that lead nowhere. Named, and left unpriced. We do not invent a number for someone's time.

The year figure is the monthly total times twelve. Our fee should land between 10 and 30 percent of it. The design firm: SAR 4,667 plus SAR 11,550 a month, SAR 194,604 a year, against an engagement of SAR 39,375, which is 20 percent. If a deal cannot produce a figure like that from numbers the client actually gave, the diagnosis is not finished and the proposal is not ready.

## Numbers quoted as proof, across the calls

Use these as given; they recur consistently.

- A design and fit-out company went from one project every two months to two a month, roughly 4x revenue, inside the first three months.
- A contracting company signed four projects worth about USD 2 million in its first two months.
- A campaign spending USD 500 produced 104 enquiries, 35 meetings, 28 quotations and three signed deals worth USD 147,000.
- A campaign spending USD 3,000 a month produced signed contracts worth USD 225,000 to 252,000.
- A high-end design company that could never reach its buyer bracket on social reached USD 300,000.
- A company four months old with no signed work closed its first project within two weeks of starting.
